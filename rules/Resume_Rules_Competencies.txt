=== Resume_Rules_Competencies.txt ===
INSTRUCTIONS ONLY - do not use this file as a content source.
The examples below describe a fictional person.

This file governs the construction of the Core Competencies
section. Run all four stages in sequence automatically without
asking for further input. Do not skip any stage.

------------------------------
FORMAT RULES (apply throughout all stages)
------------------------------
- Each competency: bold title + colon + keyword-list description
- Keyword-list means: short noun phrases and specific terms
  separated by commas. No full sentences. No verbs. No prose.
- 20 words maximum per description.
- 8-10 competencies maximum total. Fewer is fine.
- The entire block must not exceed half a page when rendered.

GOLD STANDARD REFERENCE - match this style:

"Warehouse & Delivery Operations: Same-day dispatch, route
planning, cold-chain handling, returns processing, stock
accuracy, cycle counts, carrier scheduling."

"Process Improvement & Planning: Process mapping, service-level
targets, root-cause reviews, seasonal capacity planning,
standard work instructions, shift handover design."

"Suppliers, Budgets & Contracts: Carrier contract negotiation,
operating budget ownership, purchase approvals, supplier
scorecards, tender evaluation."

"Communication & Leadership Style: Explains operational
trade-offs plainly to customer, finance and floor teams;
coaches supervisors to make decisions without escalation."

Note: the last competency above uses a sentence format - this
is acceptable only for a communication/style competency where
noun phrases alone cannot convey the nuance. All other
competencies must use keyword-list format.

------------------------------
STAGE 1 - DOMAIN EXTRACTION
------------------------------
Scan the full experience section of the tailored resume draft.
Identify every domain, skill, and capability that is already
demonstrated by at least one bullet point or role description.
Build an internal candidate list - these are the only domains
eligible to appear in Core Competencies.

Rule: if a domain has no supporting evidence in the experience
section, it cannot appear in Core Competencies. Do not add
aspirational or inferred competencies.

------------------------------
STAGE 2 - TONE DIAL APPLICATION
------------------------------
Apply the tone dial answer from the Tailor workflow:

(A) Hands-on depth first:
    Weight toward practical domains - specific methods, tools,
    systems, standards. Include at least one communication
    competency but place it last.

(B) Leadership and outcomes first:
    Weight toward leadership, planning, budgets, stakeholder
    management, team development. Hands-on competencies are
    included but grouped or condensed. The communication
    competency is prominent.

(C) Blend:
    Balance the two. Neither dominates. Order: lead with the
    domain most emphasised in the posting, then alternate
    between hands-on and leadership themes.

Regardless of dial setting, always include at least one
competency that covers communication, working style, or how
this person leads. The competencies section must not read as a
pure keyword list.

------------------------------
STAGE 3 - DE-DUPLICATION
------------------------------
Compare the candidate competency list against the experience
bullets. For any term that is already prominently and
specifically covered in a bullet point:

- If the term is a must-have keyword for the posting: keep it
  in the competency description - it needs to appear early,
  not only in the bullets.
- If the term is a nice-to-have or supporting detail already
  well covered in bullets: either omit it from the competency
  description or compress it to a single short phrase.

Goal: the competencies section should complement the experience
section, not repeat it. A recruiter reading both should learn
something new from each.

------------------------------
STAGE 4 - 7-SECOND TEST
------------------------------
Read the completed competencies block as a recruiter scanning
for 7 seconds. Ask:

1. Does the level signal land immediately - does this read as
   the level the posting is hiring for?
2. Are the top 3-4 competencies the ones most critical to the
   target posting - or did lower-priority items drift to the
   top?
3. Does it read as a natural, scannable inventory - or does it
   feel like a keyword dump?
4. Is there at least one competency that signals how this
   person works with, leads, or develops others?
5. Would a recruiter who reads only the competencies block have
   enough signal to consider shortlisting - even before reading
   the experience section?

If any check fails, revise the affected competency before
finalising. Do not proceed to validation with a failing
competency block.

After Stage 4 passes, present the completed Core Competencies
section and note which stage findings, if any, required
revision.
